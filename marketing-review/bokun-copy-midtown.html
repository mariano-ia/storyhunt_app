This is not a tour. It's a protocol.

No guide. No group. No headset. Just your phone, a chat, and a signal someone buried in the busiest square mile on earth — left for the one person who would actually stop and read it.

Midtown is the part of New York everyone moves through and nobody sees. Eight million people cross these blocks with their heads down, late for something, photographing the same three corners. The Protocol is everything they walk past — the things hiding in plain sight between Grand Central and Bryant Park, under the lions, behind the facades nobody looks up at.

What you'll do

Over about two hours you'll move through Midtown — Grand Central, the New York Public Library, Bryant Park, and the side streets most visitors never turn down — following a thread of clues that turns the city's most photographed square mile into a puzzle only you are solving.

Along the way:

  • The whispering gallery beneath Grand Central, where you can stand in opposite corners and speak in secrets
  • Patience and Fortitude — the two lions guarding the library, and what they were named for
  • Bryant Park, and what stood on that ground long before anyone laid out the lawn
  • The corners and lobbies the eight million walk past every day without ever looking up

How it works

The experience runs as a chat on your phone. You walk. It speaks. It asks you what you see, where you are, who you are. The story moves at your pace, not on a schedule — stop for a coffee or a drink, and pick it up exactly where you left off.

What you'll need

  • A phone with mobile data
  • Comfortable shoes and about two hours
  • Headphones recommended
  • One access link covers your whole group, up to 8 players

Not for everyone

If you want a guide pointing at monuments and a checklist to tick off, this isn't it. The Protocol is quieter and stranger than that — a story you decode by walking, in the one neighborhood that's convinced it has no secrets left. It does. Start whenever you're ready.
